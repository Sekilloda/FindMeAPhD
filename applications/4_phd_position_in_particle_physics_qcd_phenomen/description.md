Applications are invited for a doctoral position (up to 5 years) at the 
Division of Theoretical Physics
 (Particle Physics and Cosmology Group) of the 
Rudjer Bošković Institute
 (Zagreb, Croatia).The position is full-time and includes enrolment in a PhD programme.
Requirements are an MSc degree (or equivalent) in physics, an interest in particle physics, and proficiency in English.
Hadrons, such as protons, neutrons and mesons, consist of quarks and gluons whose interactions are described by quantum chromodynamics (QCD). However, a detailed understanding of how quarks and gluons form bound states and determine hadron properties remains a major challenge. Understanding hadron structure is also essential for precision measurements at particle colliders, including searches for physics beyond the Standard Model.
This is a particularly exciting time for hadron structure research, with a wealth of experimental data already available and more expected from Jefferson Lab (JLab), as well as new opportunities offered by the future Electron-Ion Collider (EIC) at Brookhaven National Laboratory.
The research will focus on theoretical and phenomenological studies of hadron structure, particularly hard exclusive processes and generalized parton distributions (GPDs). The work will involve analytical calculations, phenomenological modelling, and the development of computational tools, making use of AI-assisted methods.
The research is also connected to HADRON-2030, a four-year Horizon Europe project launched in October 2026 to advance our understanding of the strong interaction and hadron physics. The project brings together an international community of theoretical and experimental research groups. The PhD work will also offer opportunities for collaboration with researchers at institutions abroad.
