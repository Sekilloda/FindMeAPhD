## 1. Strategic Match & Research Angle

### Core Overlap Analysis
The University of York group (Farkas, Pusey, Weigert) operates at the mathematical and foundational core of quantum information: **convex optimization, Bell non-locality, device-independence, contextuality, and mutually unbiased bases (MUBs)**. Your Master’s thesis on QCD phenomenology under the Lund string model, specifically your design of a **quantum algorithm for hadronization histories** and **rank indistinguishability witness circuits**, sits at a highly synergistic intersection:

| York Research Focus | Your Thesis Component | Strategic Bridge |
|---------------------|----------------------|------------------|
| **Convex Optimization** | Witness circuit construction & rank indistinguishability | Quantum witnesses are inherently convex optimization problems over state spaces. Your algorithmic experience directly translates to optimizing Bell/compatibility witnesses. |
| **Bell Non-Locality & Device-Independence** | Quantum algorithm for hadronization histories | Both require simulating complex, high-dimensional quantum correlations under physical constraints. Your background in modeling non-perturbative quantum systems provides a unique computational lens for DI protocols. |
| **Causality & Contextuality** | Lund string fragmentation dynamics | String hadronization involves causal ordering of particle production and contextual measurement outcomes. You can frame this as a natural testing ground for foundational causality models. |
| **Quantum Cryptography & MUBs** | Rank indistinguishability & quantum circuit design | MUBs and cryptographic protocols rely on geometric structures in Hilbert space. Your circuit-level intuition for indistinguishability witnesses maps cleanly to protocol security proofs. |

### Pitch Strategy
1. **Reframe QCD as a Strength, Not a Detour:** Position your particle physics background as advanced training in **non-perturbative quantum many-body dynamics, Monte Carlo simulation, and algorithmic complexity**. Emphasize that modern QCD phenomenology requires the same mathematical machinery (convex sets, optimization, information-theoretic bounds) used in York’s foundational work.
2. **Lead with Witnesses & Optimization:** Your "rank indistinguishability witness circuits" is your strongest asset. Explicitly state how this experience equips you to tackle convex optimization over quantum state spaces, a cornerstone of Bell non-locality and device-independent certification.
3. **Address the Funding/Geography Note Strategically:** Acknowledge the mixed funded/unfunded status by expressing readiness to apply for standard UKRI/EPSRC studentships (which historically meet your >€2,000/month net threshold). Note that while the UK falls outside your top geographic priority, the academic prestige, research alignment, and streamlined administrative path for EU citizens make it a compelling target.

## 4. Targeted Interview Questions

Prepare these for direct engagement with Prof. Farkas, Prof. Pusey, or Prof. Weigert. Each is designed to demonstrate technical depth, research foresight, and alignment with the group’s methodology.

1. **On Convex Optimization & Witnesses:**  
   *"Your group frequently employs convex optimization to certify Bell non-locality and device-independent correlations. Given my background in constructing rank indistinguishability witness circuits for hadronization histories, I’m curious how you approach the computational scaling of witness optimization in high-dimensional or noisy regimes. Are there ongoing efforts to integrate variational or quantum-native optimization methods to accelerate this process?"*

2. **On Causality & Contextuality:**  
   *"In my thesis work, I modeled causal ordering in string fragmentation and analyzed contextual measurement dependencies. How does the group currently formalize causality constraints in foundational models, and do you see opportunities to apply algorithmic verification techniques—similar to those used in quantum information protocols—to test or bound causal structures in operational theories?"*

3. **On MUBs & Quantum Cryptography:**  
   *"Mutually unbiased bases play a central role in both foundational studies and cryptographic security proofs. Are there active projects exploring the geometric or algebraic structure of MUBs for constructing robust device-independent randomness expansion or key distribution protocols? I’d be particularly interested in how convex geometry intersects with basis selection in these contexts."*

4. **On Funding & Research Trajectory:**  
   *"The vacancy mentions both funded and unfunded positions. Could you outline how the department typically supports candidates in securing external studentships (e.g., EPSRC, UKRI, or international fellowships) for Autumn 2027 entry? Additionally, how does the group balance foundational theoretical work with computational implementation, and where do you see the most immediate need for candidates with strong algorithmic and analytical training?"*
