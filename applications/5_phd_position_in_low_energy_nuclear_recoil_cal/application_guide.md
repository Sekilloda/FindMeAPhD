## 1. Strategic Match & Research Angle

### Overlap Analysis
The vacancy is fundamentally **experimental/computational**, centered on cryogenic detector calibration, Geant4-based Monte Carlo simulation, statistical inference, and optional machine learning/DAQ development. Your background is **theoretical/computational**, anchored in QCD phenomenology, the Lund string model, and quantum algorithm design. The apparent disciplinary gap is bridged by three core transferable competencies:

1. **Stochastic Process Modeling:** The Lund string model describes hadronization as a highly non-perturbative, stochastic fragmentation process. This maps directly to modeling low-energy nuclear recoils, phonon/photon generation in cryogenic calorimeters, and detector response functions. Your experience simulating complex particle histories translates seamlessly to Geant4-based simulation workflows and calibration benchmarking.
2. **Algorithmic & Statistical Rigor:** Your work on rank indistinguishability witness circuits and quantum algorithms for hadronization histories demonstrates advanced proficiency in probability theory, statistical inference, and algorithmic optimization. These are the exact mathematical foundations required for Bayesian calibration, uncertainty quantification, and likelihood-based analysis in rare-event searches.
3. **Computational Physics Mindset:** Your preference for computational research aligns with the vacancy’s emphasis on Python/C++, Monte Carlo simulation, data analysis, and optional ML integration. You are not pivoting away from theory; you are **translating theoretical/computational expertise into applied astroparticle physics**.

### Pitch Strategy
Frame your application around the narrative:  
> *"From stochastic hadronization modeling to low-energy recoil calibration: leveraging advanced computational frameworks, statistical inference, and algorithmic design for next-generation rare-event searches."*

Emphasize that your Master’s thesis required you to:
- Design algorithms that sample high-dimensional stochastic spaces (hadronization histories) → directly analogous to simulating detector responses and calibration datasets.
- Develop witness circuits and statistical tests → directly applicable to calibration validation, background rejection, and confidence interval construction.
- Work at the intersection of quantum information and particle physics → positions you to contribute to emerging ML/quantum-inspired methods in detector R&D and data acquisition.

Acknowledge the experimental shift explicitly but frame it as a deliberate, strategic move toward **applied computational astroparticle physics**. Highlight your openness to hardware-software co-design, Geant4, and DAQ systems as natural extensions of your algorithmic and simulation background.
