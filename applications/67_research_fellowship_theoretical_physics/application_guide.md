### Core Overlap Analysis

### How to Pitch the QCD + Quantum Computing Intersection
1. **Frame Hadronization as a Quantum Simulation Problem**: Classically, the Lund string model relies on stochastic parton branching and string fragmentation, which scales poorly for high-multiplicity final states. Position your quantum algorithm for hadronization histories as a pathway to **exact sampling of non-perturbative QCD phase space** or **quantum-enhanced Monte Carlo acceleration**.
2. **Elevate Rank Indistinguishability Witness Circuits**: Connect this to quantum information theory and particle statistics. Emphasize how witness circuits can detect entanglement structures in multi-particle final states, potentially offering new observables for ALICE/LHCb correlation measurements.
3. **Bridge Theory & Experiment**: CERN’s Theory Department values work that informs experimental interpretation. Highlight your willingness to develop phenomenological tools that can be interfaced with detector simulations (e.g., GEANT4, Pythia) or used to design quantum-native observables.
4. **Emphasize Analytical Rigor + Computational Innovation**: CERN TH does not seek pure coders or pure formalists. Stress your ability to derive analytical QCD results, validate them against lattice/phenomenological benchmarks, and translate them into quantum circuit architectures.

- Place **Research Experience** immediately after Education.
- Use bullet points starting with strong action verbs: `Developed`, `Designed`, `Validated`, `Collaborated`, `Published`.
- Quantify impact where possible: e.g., `Reduced sampling complexity by O(N)`, `Benchmarked against Pythia 8`, `Achieved X% fidelity on quantum simulator`.
- Keep CV to 2–3 pages. CERN reviewers scan quickly; prioritize relevance over completeness.
