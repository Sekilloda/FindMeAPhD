## Original Vacancy Description

```text
To strengthen the international 𝜏SPECT collaboration, we invite applications for 1 PhD position, 75 % EG13,
to develop and investigate novel techniques to control critical systematic bias effects related to the determination
of the free neutron lifetime based on ultracold neutron (UCN) stored in a fully magnetic trap.
The lifetime of the free neutron is a key observable in low-energy tests of the Standard Model of Particle
Physics (SM) which provides a theoretical description of the free neutron decay with only three
(low-energy) parameters and observables to very high precision and accuracy: The CKM matrix element
Vud, the ratio of axial-vector and vector coupling strength, and the free neutron lifetime.
Any tension within this description could be a tantalizing hint to physics beyond the SM.
The 𝜏SPECT experiment confines UCNs in a fully magnetic trap and counts the number of UCNs surviving
present but variable storage times. The trap is formed by a region of a magnetic field minimum
surrounded by strong positive magnetic field gradients. 𝜏SPECT uses the unique double-spin-flip loading
technique to confine low-field seeking UCN in the volume around the magnetic field minimum. The
experiment is installed at the UCN source of the Paul Scherrer Institute, Switzerland, and is currently
taking scientific data.
The novel spin-flip loading scheme for the fully magnetic trap suppresses critical systematic bias effects
of historic neutron lifetime experiments, e.g., interaction with material surfaces, and opens a new class
of possible optimizations of neutron lifetime experiments based on UCNs. This thesis will investigate
the phase space evolution of UCNs in the fully magnetic trap and the impact of tailored radio-frequency
pulses through Monte-Carlo simulation and analytical calculations. So-called marginally trapped UCNs
are barely confined in the trapping potential and are a possible source of bias towards a lower UCN
storage time. Based on the findings, novel radio-frequency systems shall be developed and built to control
marginally trapped UCNs in the current and future experiments. Through the participation in the ongoing
data taking phases and the analysis of the recorded data, the candidate will be embedded in the
collaboration and will become deeply familiar with the physics of UCNs, high- and ultra-high vacuum
technology, cryogenic setups, neutron detection, data acquisition systems, and extended data analysis.
This position is part of the Cluster of Excellence PRISMA++ and the ERC Consolidator Grant NuLife.
```
