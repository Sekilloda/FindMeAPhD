Project description
Subject: Theoretical Physics
We invite applications for a PhD position in the theory of quantum sensing at the Department of Physics (Fysikum), Stockholm University. The project will be carried out within the research group led by Sofia Qvarfort and will be based at Nordita as part of the Wallenberg Centre for Quantum Technology (WACQT). The successful candidate will join a collaborative and international research environment in quantum physics, as well as the WACQT graduate school, with opportunities to interact with both theoretical and experimental researchers.
Quantum sensing is a rapidly developing quantum technology that aims to exploit quantum effects to achieve enhanced precision in measurements of weak forces, accelerations, gravitational effects, and other physical parameters. Candidate platforms span a broad range, from individual atoms and superconducting devices to mechanical resonators operating in the quantum regime.
A central question in quantum sensing is what physical resources can provide a genuine sensing advantage, and under which realistic conditions that advantage survives. Improvements in sensitivity can arise through the preparation of non-classical quantum states, changes in the dynamics by which a parameter is encoded, suitable measurement strategies, or by connecting multiple sensors into distributed quantum sensing networks. Understanding the interplay between these mechanisms, particularly in the presence of noise and decoherence, remains an important theoretical challenge.
This PhD project will investigate fundamental limits and new protocols for quantum sensing using tools from mathematical physics, quantum information theory, open quantum systems, and quantum metrology. Possible research directions include nonlinear and anharmonic quantum dynamics, generation and use of non-Gaussian states, distributed quantum sensing, and the role of realistic measurements and environmental noise. The project will be motivated by experimentally relevant quantum technologies, with an emphasis on mechanical resonators in the quantum regime. An important goal is to identify which physical resources and protocols can lead to meaningful improvements in sensitivity under realistic experimental constraints.
