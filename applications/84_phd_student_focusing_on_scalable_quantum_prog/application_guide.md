## 1. Strategic Match & Research Angle

### Core Overlap Analysis
This vacancy sits at the intersection of **formal methods, programming language theory, and quantum computing**. While the host department is Computer Science/IT rather than Physics, the project explicitly targets *quantum program verification* for hybrid architectures and quantum error correction (QEC). Your Master’s thesis on QCD phenomenology under the Lund string model, combined with a quantum algorithm for hadronization histories and rank indistinguishability witness circuits, provides a highly relevant, albeit non-traditional, foundation.

**Key Bridges:**
- **Witness Circuits → Property Verification:** Rank indistinguishability witness circuits are inherently verification tools. They test whether a quantum state satisfies specific entanglement or symmetry properties. This maps directly to the vacancy’s focus on formal verification, automated theorem proving, and checking quantum program correctness.
- **Hadronization Simulation → Hybrid Control Loops:** Simulating hadronization histories requires managing complex classical-quantum control flows, state evolution, and probabilistic sampling. This mirrors the hybrid quantum-classical architectures the project aims to verify.
- **Mathematical Rigor → Formal Semantics:** Your background in QFT, tensor networks, and Monte Carlo methods demonstrates the mathematical maturity required for formal semantics, complex arithmetic, and state-space reasoning.

### Strategic Pitching Angle
Do **not** position yourself as a particle phenomenologist seeking a quantum computing pivot. Instead, frame yourself as a **theoretical physicist who has already operationalized verification-adjacent techniques in quantum algorithm design**, now seeking to formalize and scale those methods within a rigorous computer science framework.

**Narrative Arc:**
1. **Domain Challenge:** Complex quantum systems (e.g., hadronization, QEC) generate state spaces and control flows that outpace ad-hoc simulation.
2. **Your Intervention:** You designed quantum algorithms and witness circuits that inherently require property checking, circuit validation, and hybrid classical-quantum coordination.
3. **The Gap:** Current verification tools lack scalability, automation, and guarantees for these hybrid, high-dimensional systems.
4. **Your Fit:** Your mathematical training, circuit design experience, and exposure to formal property checking align with the group’s mission to merge classical verification/PL theory with quantum program semantics.

### Why Uppsala & This Group?
- The department’s leadership in formal verification (UPPAAL, Concuerror, Nidhugg) and programming language semantics offers the exact methodological toolkit you need to transition from domain-specific simulation to generalizable verification frameworks.
- Sweden’s domestic hiring status (EU citizenship) and standard PhD employment conditions (~30,000 SEK/month gross, comfortably exceeding your €2,000 net threshold) align with your logistical and financial requirements.

## 4. Targeted Interview Questions

Prepare these to demonstrate deep engagement with the project’s technical scope and the group’s research ecosystem:

1. **On Hybrid Verification Architectures:**  
   > "Given the project’s focus on hybrid quantum-classical control loops, how does the group plan to handle the verification of classical feedback mechanisms that depend on probabilistic quantum measurements? Are there plans to integrate stochastic model checking or probabilistic programming semantics into the verification pipeline?"

2. **On Scaling & State-Space Explosion:**  
   > "As quantum hardware scales to thousands of qubits, state-space verification becomes computationally intractable. Does the project intend to leverage symmetry reduction, abstraction techniques, or domain-specific languages to mitigate state-space explosion, particularly when verifying quantum error correction codes?"

3. **On Tool Integration & Formal Semantics:**  
   > "The department has a strong track record with tools like UPPAAL and Concuerror. Are there concrete plans to extend these frameworks to handle quantum-specific semantics (e.g., superposition, entanglement, or non-commutative arithmetic), or will the project focus on developing a dedicated quantum verification DSL that interfaces with existing classical verifiers?"

4. **On Bridging Formal Guarantees & Quantum Probabilism:**  
   > "Formal verification traditionally provides deterministic guarantees, whereas quantum programs are inherently probabilistic. How does the project envision reconciling formal correctness criteria with the statistical nature of quantum measurements, especially when scaling to fault-tolerant architectures?"
